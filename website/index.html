Tabs you aren’t looking at go to sleep.

Kakangkung is a free web browser for Windows, written in Rust. It draws its own interface and freezes the tabs you left behind, so the page in front of you gets the memory.

What happens to a tab you leave

0 s 30 s click
  1. You switch to another tab. The one you left keeps its scripts, timers and video running, as in any browser.
  2. Kakangkung freezes it. On the Maximum setting, which new profiles use, a hidden tab freezes after 30 seconds. Tabs playing sound, in a call or pinned are left alone.
  3. You click it again. It wakes where you left it. The memory it gave back went to the page you were using.

Every built-in page is drawn by Kakangkung, not loaded as a website.

Settings, History, the task manager and the rest are native Direct2D screens. Opening one starts no page process. These are real screenshots.

Also built in

An ad blocker that is on from the first start
EasyList and EasyPrivacy network rules. The shield in the toolbar turns blocking off for one site.
Passwords sealed by Windows
Saved passwords are encrypted with your Windows account. Showing or exporting one asks Windows Hello. Passkeys work.
PDFs in a tab
Search, rotate, two-page view, print, and drawing on local files.
Developer tools
F12 opens them in a panel at the bottom of the tab.
Import from other browsers
History, bookmarks and extensions from Chrome, Edge, Brave and Firefox.
Updates that check themselves
New versions download in the background and are verified against their SHA-256 before installing.

Sync goes through your own Google Drive

Sign in with Google and Kakangkung keeps bookmarks, history, open tabs, settings and your extension list the same on every PC. It stores them in the hidden app folder of your Drive.

  • It can see only the files it created there, never your own Drive files.
  • Nothing goes to a Kakangkung server. There isn’t one.
  • Passwords are never synced.
  • Signing out deletes its token and asks Google to revoke it.
